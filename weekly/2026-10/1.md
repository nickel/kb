Answers to "Open questions to Tech" (section 08)

1. Can the platform hold several price lists at once, each with its own effective date, and keep superseded ones?
Yes for dates and history, no for the "without a release" part.
- What works today: each product and market can have several price lists, each with an effective date. The one in force is the latest whose date has passed. Old ones are never changed or deleted.
- Offers and loans keep their list: every offer and loan records which list priced it. An accepted offer is priced against that same list even if a newer one takes effect in between, which is the "issued offers hold their rate" rule in section 07.
- What is missing:
  - There's no screen or upload for a new list, so today a new list needs an engineering change.
  - There's no approval step, so Valnor can't approve a version before it goes live.
  - Today's list only varies the rate by term and by a risk label that's empty everywhere. It is not a six-key matrix.

Building the matrix store means adding all three: a matrix import, a maker/checker approval, and the six-key format. Effective dating and history carry over from what exists.

2. Can a pricing group and an experiment id be stored on the application and come out in the data?
Not today, but the place for it exists.
- Where it goes: each application already keeps a small set of fields that can only be written once and that pin the offer the customer was shown: the term, the price list, the risk label. The group and the rule that assigned it belong there too, so they can't be changed after the offer.
- What we'd add: carry them onto the loan, and include them in the reporting extract for conversion, take-up, arrears and margin by group.
- Question back: which data source do you read "the data" from, so we put the group where you will look?

3. What happens today when a lookup finds no matching row?
Today's model has no rows, and its behaviour is the opposite of the spec's "no default".
- Risk rate missing: a missing risk-graded rate falls back to the rate for the term, then to the product's single flat rate, by design. That keeps the product priced while the risk scale is undecided.
- Amount or term outside the product: the broker gets a decline ("no terms available"), and we raise no alert.
- Our own pricing inconsistency: the application is left unanswered and escalated to an operator, never sent to Lendo as a decline. We don't tell Lendo "no" for something that is our fault.

The matrix will follow your rule: no fallback, no nearest neighbour, no offer plus an alert. One point to decide: should a matrix miss be reported to Lendo as a decline, or left unanswered with an alert to us? It's a gap in our own configuration, not a decision about the customer, so we'd recommend unanswered plus an alert.

We also propose to check completeness when a matrix is imported: refuse a version with any missing or duplicate combination, or a rate above the legal cap. A missing row then can't reach production at all, and the runtime miss only covers an input value outside the matrix.

4. Where is a generated SECCI stored, and how is it retrieved per application?
- The figures: every figure in the SECCI is frozen on the contract record created for the application (draft contract, one per application). It's created once from the priced decision and never recalculated. The screen and the signed agreement both read from it, so they cannot disagree.
- What the customer saw: each time the SECCI is shown, we record where (checkout, app, account) and a fingerprint of exactly what was displayed.
- The signed agreement: stored as a finished PDF in the document archive, filed against the contra
- Not built yet: a stored PDF of the SECCI itself, delivered on a durable medium (a format the cusked as reg-008, and the rendering and archive it needs already exist.
- Lendo: gets figures from the same frozen record, not a file, which matches section 05.

5. Does anything about the proposed matrix layout not work for us?
The layout works: 117,504 rows is small for a database, and an ID built from the keys is a good chle before the first file:
1. Decimal comma. "0,0–5,0" in a comma-separated file collides with the column separator. Please use a decimal point, or a semicolon separator, and state which.
2. Credit score. Which attribute is it (we assume UC's risk forecast), where do we read it, and how does it round to the 0.1 step? What happens to an applicant above 5.0%?
3. Grid against the product. The agreed product lends 100,000–300,000 over 12–144 months, and the seed also has a 180-month term. The matrix starts at 150,000 with no 180. Should loans under 150,000 and the 180-month term stop being offered, or should the matrix grow?
4. Purposes. Today we accept five purposes: vehicle, debt consolidation, home improvement, leisure, other consumption. The matrix prices three, so leisure and other-consumption applications would pass every check and then find no price. Narrow what we accept, add rows, or map them? And does house_and_home mean our home improvement?
5. Owned property. No field states it cleanly. Lendo's "house" doesn't say owned or rented. Which source decides: the application, a UC attribute, or both with a priority rule?
6. Rate format. State it as a percentage with fixed decimals (for example 6.95, two decimals). We'll reject any row above the legal cap at import rather than at offer time.
7. ID format. In UL-SE-A-150-012-25-Y-VEH the term is zero-padded and the score isn't, and only VEH is defined. Fixed widths and a code for each purpose would make the ID safe to sort and join on.
8. Group B while the test is off. Should a matrix be complete for B even when everyone is in A? We'd require it, so turning the test on never meets a missing price.
