If your team has the technical capacity to build true IFRS 9 Effective Interest Rate (EIR) calculation logic from Day 0, then relying on the shortcut model is indeed a clear “NO”.
Using the 1 September shortcut (contractual interest + straight-line fee amortization) is an operational compromise, not an accounting best practice. If the technical barrier to building true EIR doesn’t exist, choosing the shortcut creates unnecessary risk without any benefit.

### Why relying on the shortcut (when you can build true IFRS 9) is a clear “NO”

1. Zero Audit & Policy Exposure: Under IFRS 9 paragraph 5.4.1, interest revenue must be calculated using the effective interest method applied to the gross carrying amount. Origination fees and direct broker costs must adjust the EIR rather than amortize separately. Building this natively from Day 0 provides 100% compliance out of the box, eliminating the risk of external auditors rejecting an IAS 8.8 materiality defense as your loan book scales.
2. Prevents Costly Re-Platforming Debt: Upgrading an accounting engine post-launch is notoriously painful. Moving from straight-line to compounding EIR later requires historical portfolio re-evaluations, retrospective catch-up adjustments, or complex transition disclosures
3. Institutional & Funding Readiness: Credit facility providers, debt syndicates, securitization partners, and regulatory bodies (such as FINREP) expect standard IFRS 9 amortised cost accounting. Having native EIR accounting from launch makes due diligence significantly cleaner.

### Summary Recommendation

The only scenario where the shortcut model is acceptable is as a temporary stopgap if building true EIR would delay your product launch or exceed engineering resources. If you have the engineering bandwidth to build true IFRS 9 EIR from inception, build true IFRS 9 EIR from Day 0.

If your entity reports under IFRS (or Swedish accounting rules referencing IFRS 9, such as FFFS 2014:1 / ÅRKL):

- The 1 September Decision is an Immaterial Departure: The shortcut model (contractual interest + straight-line fees) does not comply with the strict mechanics of IFRS 9. Audit Defense Requirement: To maintain this shortcut model for operational simplicity, the company must prepare a materiality impact assessment. This document must demonstrate that:
- The delta between the shortcut method and true IFRS 9 EIR is quantitatively immaterial across the loan portfolio.
- The departure was not intentionally introduced to manipulate or smooth earnings, financial position, or cash flows (satisfying the second condition of IAS 8.8)

## How Klarna books its fees (Klarna Bank AB, Annual Report 2023)

Klarna is a Swedish bank and follows the same accounting rules we will: IFRS, the Swedish accounts act for credit institutions (ÅRKL) and FI’s rules (FFFS 2008:25). Its 2023 annual report explains how it books the money it earns, and it splits that money into two groups.

1. Money that is part of the price of the credit

Klarna spreads this over the life of the loan using the effective interest method and shows it as interest income. It includes:
- interest on loans;
- fees for giving credit, which Klarna calls “an integral part of the effective interest rate”;
- money merchants pay to fund promotional loans, such as 0% offers. Klarna used to book this as commission income. In 2023 it moved it to interest income, spread over the loan, and restated its 2022 figures (SEK 221 million).

2. Money that pays for a service

Klarna books this as commission income under IFRS 15, not spread with the effective interest method. It includes:
- invoice and statement fees paid by consumers, booked when the invoice or statement is sent;
- merchant commission for the payment and credit service, booked over the merchant contract as sales happen.

## What this means for us

- Our broker commission and any setup fee are part of the price of the credit. The rules put them inside the effective interest rate, not on a straight-line schedule. Klarna’s report does not mention broker commissions, but IFRS 9 names them directly (paragraph B5.4.8).
- Our invoice fees and normal merchant fees can stay outside the effective interest rate.
- Merchant money that pays for our 0% plans may need to be spread over the loan, as Klarna does.

This is based on one company’s 2023 report. The final decision is ours, together with our auditor.

Source: Klarna Bank AB, Annual Report 2023, note 2, section 6 “Interest and commissions”, and the note on reclassified comparatives.
